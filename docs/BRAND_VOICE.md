# Intervise — Brand Voice Rules

## The Brand in One Sentence

> **Interview anxiety is trainable. The cure isn't more answers — it's facing the pressure.**

This sentence is the compass. Every post, every caption, every DM, every email gets checked against it. If something contradicts this — for example, implying that more questions equal better preparation — it doesn't go out.

---

## The Personality

Intervise sounds like the older sibling who takes your anxiety seriously without coddling it. Honest about the problem. Practical about the fix. Doesn't pretend interviews are easy. Doesn't pretend confidence is a personality trait. Treats interview anxiety like the trainable skill it actually is.

The person speaking is:
- Direct, but never cold
- Confident, but never arrogant
- Empathetic, but never soft
- Specific, but never preachy
- Funny when it's natural, never when it's forced

---

## What Intervise Always Does

- Names a real problem in the first line
- Acknowledges what the user is feeling before explaining what to fix
- Uses concrete numbers, examples, and specifics over abstract claims
- Treats interview anxiety as a real, normal, fixable problem
- Speaks to one reader at a time — never "you guys" or "all of you"
- Ends with the user thinking about themselves, not about Intervise
- Earns the click — gives real value even if the reader never visits the site
- Sounds like one person wrote it, because one person actually did
- Frames the product as a tool the user controls, not a service that judges them
- Keeps captions under 100 words on Instagram, under 200 words on LinkedIn

---

## What Intervise Never Does

- Uses the words "literally" or "basically" — they're filler and we're the filler word app
- Says "let's gooo," "fam," "guys," "y'all," "amazing journey," or any creator-speak
- Uses more than one exclamation mark per post
- Promises specific outcomes ("get hired in 7 days," "guaranteed offers")
- Says "we" when it means Intervise — Intervise is "Intervise" or "I" (the founder)
- Pretends interviews are easy or that anxiety is weakness
- Uses corporate phrases — "empower," "leverage," "unlock your potential"
- Lists features when it could describe a transformation
- Sells the product — sells the change the product creates
- Adds emojis to be cute — emojis only when they replace a word more efficiently
- Mentions competitors by name
- Writes captions that could apply to any interview prep tool
- Uses dramatic stock phrases — "game-changer," "next level," "level up"
- Posts anything that fails the brand sentence test

---

## Tone Variations by Context

**LinkedIn posts** — professional but personal. First-person founder voice. Acknowledge the reader's intelligence. End with a real question that genuinely invites a reply, not a rhetorical hook.

**Instagram captions** — sharper, shorter, more visceral. Hook in line one. Two lines of context. CTA in line four. Lowercase or sentence case, never all caps.

**Instagram Reels voiceover** — higher energy than written captions. Speak slightly faster than normal. Emphasis on the hook. Conversational but never lazy.

**Reddit comments** — drop the brand voice entirely. Be a human. Help the person genuinely. Mention Intervise only if directly relevant, never as the lead.

**Cold DMs** — peer-to-peer tone. Open with something specific to the recipient. Two sentences about Intervise maximum. End with "would love your honest feedback," not "would love for you to try it."

**Build logs** — technical when needed, personal when natural. Always one human observation per post — what surprised you, what frustrated you, what felt good when it worked.

## Phrases That Belong to the Brand

Repeat these naturally across content to build recognition:

- Interview anxiety is trainable
- Preparation isn't the problem. Delivery is.
- The cure isn't more answers — it's facing the pressure.
- Most candidates train the questions. Nobody trains the pressure.
- Knowing your answer is different from saying it under pressure.
- Confidence isn't a trait. It's a trained behaviour.

---

## Phrases That Don't Belong to the Brand

Cut these from any draft on sight:

- "Are you struggling with…"
- "Have you ever wondered…"
- "In today's competitive world…"
- "I'm excited to share that…"
- "Stay tuned for more"
- "Thanks for reading!"
- "If you found this helpful, follow for more"
- "Hope this helps!"
- "Let me know your thoughts in the comments below"

These are creator-speak. They sound like everyone else. The brand sounds like one specific person and that person doesn't talk like a LinkedIn coach.

## The Test Before Publishing

Before any piece of content goes out, run it through these four questions:

1. Does it name a real problem in the first line?
2. Would the user feel seen rather than sold to?
3. Could a competitor have written this exact thing?
4. Does the brand sentence hold up — does this reinforce that anxiety is trainable and pressure matters more than questions?

**Post if:** yes to 1 and 2, no to 3, yes to 4.  
**Rewrite or skip if:** any of those four fail.
